## Who are the known multi-unit or multi-city lobster roll concepts in the US?

### Takeaway
There are two clear national leaders — Cousins Maine Lobster (food-truck-first, heavily franchised, ~85–89 units across 35 states as of 2025, aggressively adding ~20–30 units/year) and Mason's Famous Lobster Rolls (brick-and-mortar fast-casual, franchised, ~35 US locations across 15 states + DC, now expanding internationally) — plus a mid-size company-owned/licensed player, Luke's Lobster (~20–35 units depending on source, US + Japan/Singapore, revenue ~$75M). Below that tier, Red Hook Lobster Pound and The Clam Shack remain small, largely single-market/seasonal operations with no evidence of franchise or multi-city scaling. This category has proven it can scale to roughly 50–100 units nationally under a food-truck or fast-casual franchise model, which is the most relevant proxy ceiling for assessing a single-unit Miami concept's expansion potential.

## What is the price/cost volatility of Maine/North Atlantic lobster as a raw ingredient over the last 5 years, and what does that imply for margin stability at multi-unit scale?

### Takeaway
Maine dock (boat) lobster prices have been highly volatile over 2020–2025, swinging roughly between $3.97/lb and $6.71/lb year-to-year (not counting pandemic-era 2020 lows), while landed volume has fallen for four consecutive years to a 15-year low — a classic low-volume/high-price supply squeeze. Critically, multiple trade sources report that *retail/menu lobster-roll prices have kept rising even in years when wholesale lobster prices fell*, implying restaurant-level margins are not purely passthrough and operators have had some, but not unlimited, pricing power to decouple consumer prices from raw-material cost swings.

### Cited Findings
- 2021: Maine's most valuable lobster year on record — landed value $724.9 million (+75% over 2020), landings 108.0 million lbs, average dock price $6.71/lb — [Maine Governor's Office](https://www.maine.gov/governor/mills/news/2021-maine-lobster-harvest-most-valuable-history-fishery-2022-02-14)
- 2022: landings 97.9 million lbs, value $388.5 million, average dock price $3.97/lb — a sharp price drop from 2021 despite still-high volume — [search aggregation citing Maine DMR data]
- 2023: value ~$464 million (+$72 million over 2022), average price $4.95/lb, landings ~94 million lbs (then a 15-year low) — [Portland Press Herald](https://www.pressherald.com/2024/03/01/lobstermen-made-611-million-in-2023-rebounding-from-worst-year-in-a-decade/); [Maine Public](https://www.mainepublic.org/business-and-economy/2024-03-01/maine-lobster-harvest-sees-72-million-rebound-in-2023-even-as-landings-decline)
- 2024: landings ~86 million lbs (new 15-year low, -10M lbs vs 2023), but average dock price $6.14/lb (second-highest ever recorded), fishery value >$528 million — [Maine Public](https://www.mainepublic.org/business-and-economy/2025-02-28/maine-lobster-landings-hit-a-15-year-low-in-2024); [Mainebiz](https://mainebiz.biz/article/maine-lobster-catch-hits-a-6-year-low-in-2024/)
- 2025: landings 78.8 million lbs (fourth consecutive annual decline, down from 110M+ lbs in 2021), average dock price $5.85/lb (third-highest on record), total fishery value >$461 million (down >$75M vs 2024); harvesters took ~21,000 fewer fishing trips (-10% YoY), attributed partly to tariff-driven market uncertainty, a late molt limiting new-shell lobster supply, and inflation — [Maine DMR via Maine.gov](https://www.maine.gov/dmr/news/fri-03062026-1200-2025-maine-commercial-fisheries-value-again-tops-600-million); [National Fisherman](https://www.nationalfisherman.com/maine-lobster-landings-fall-for-fourth-straight-year); [Maine Morning Star](https://mainemorningstar.com/briefs/maine-lobster-haul-down-again-in-2025); [SeafoodSource](https://www.seafoodsource.com/news/supply-trade/preliminary-data-indicates-maine-s-fisheries-saw-value-drop-in-2025-on-low-lobster-landings)
- At the restaurant/consumer level: The Clam Shack's lobster roll price rose from $18.95 (2020) → $24.95 (2021) → $29.95 (2022) — a 58% increase in two years — [Robb Report, citing Clam Shack pricing history](https://robbreport.com/food-drink/dining/lobster-roll-prices-remain-high-lobster-prices-plummet-1234737916/)
- Cousins Maine Lobster's average nationwide lobster roll price rose from $17.50–$18 (2018) to roughly $21–$22 (2022) — [Robb Report](https://robbreport.com/food-drink/dining/lobster-roll-prices-remain-high-lobster-prices-plummet-1234737916/)
- Robb Report's explicit finding/headline: "Lobster Roll Prices Are Soaring, Even as the Price of Lobster Plummets" — restaurant-level lobster roll prices have continued climbing in years when wholesale dock prices for lobster meat fell, because rising costs for bait, fuel, and traps (lobstermen's input costs) are squeezing margins at the harvesting level even as menu prices rise at the restaurant level — i.e., price increases at the register are not simply passing through raw lobster cost changes, and are driven by broader inflation and real-estate/labor costs too — [Robb Report](https://robbreport.com/food-drink/dining/lobster-roll-prices-remain-high-lobster-prices-plummet-1234737916/)
- By 2025–2026, a basic Maine lobster roll commonly runs $25–$35, with reports of some locations (Hamptons, Boston-area high-end spots) approaching $50 — [Boston Globe](https://www.bostonglobe.com/2026/03/19/lifestyle/martha-stewart-resists-pricy-lobster-rolls-her-foxwoods-restaurant-boston-well-shell-out-them/); general range cross-referenced via [search aggregation]
- 2025 tariff policy was explicitly cited as a factor creating "market uncertainty" that changed lobstermen's fishing behavior and trip frequency — [National Fisherman](https://www.nationalfisherman.com/maine-lobster-landings-fall-for-fourth-straight-year)

### Inferences
- Raw lobster cost is genuinely volatile year-to-year (dock price has ranged roughly $4–$7/lb over 2021–2025, a ~70% band), and landed volume has been in a structural four-year decline, so a multi-unit lobster-roll concept sourcing North Atlantic lobster faces real, recurring input-cost risk that is not fully hedged by long-term contracts in evidence from any source found.
- However, the finding that consumer/menu prices have risen even when wholesale prices fell (Robb Report) suggests the category has demonstrated real pricing power with consumers — multi-unit operators appear to have been able to raise menu prices roughly in line with general inflation and input-cost direction rather than being forced into unsustainable margin compression, at least for brands able to position as "premium"/authentic (which aligns with the subject concept's hand-cleaned Maine lobster positioning at $14–75+ price points).
- A national or multi-market concept priced at the high end ($25-75+ per item) likely has more margin buffer to absorb raw-material volatility than a lower-priced ($14-20) entry-level item in the same menu, since premium pricing creates more headroom before consumer price resistance kicks in — this supports a barbell strategy (keep a low-price entry item for trial, but rely on premium tiers for margin protection against lobster cost swings). This is inference, not sourced.
- The 2025 tariff-driven uncertainty specifically flagged by National Fisherman is a live/current risk factor (as of this session's date, Oct 2026) that any lobster-sourcing concept should monitor — tariffs affecting lobster trade could affect sourcing costs independent of landings/supply dynamics.
