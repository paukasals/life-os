# Miami's 4.9-Star Lobster Roll Has Never Given a Single Interview — Until Now

### From a converted 1977 Airstream in Wynwood, a former professional water polo player built a 145-review Miami institution on hand-cleaned Maine lobster and word of mouth alone — with zero press, zero paid ads, and zero shortcuts.

**MIAMI, FL — [DATE], 2026** — Lobsteria, a Maine lobster roll concept operating out of a converted 1977 Airstream trailer in Miami's Wynwood neighborhood, has built a 4.9-star rating across 145 Google reviews without a single press mention, paid ad campaign, or PR outreach since opening in 2019. Today, for the first time, its chef-owner is telling the story behind it.

Pau Casals, a former professional water polo player for the Spanish national team, came to the United States in 2012 on an O-1 visa for individuals of extraordinary ability. He grew up shopping the stalls of La Boqueria market in Barcelona with his family — an upbringing where fresh, real seafood with no shortcuts was simply normal. After his athletic career, he worked restaurant weekends on the side while building a career in property management. Then the pandemic took it all away.

Casals looked at what Miami was calling a lobster roll and knew exactly what was wrong with it. He went all-in on Lobsteria.

**"There's nobody doing this the way it's supposed to be done."** [Verbatim quote Pau has used to describe his motivation — confirm before this goes to press, and see the draft continuation below for approval.]

Every roll starts with wild-caught Maine lobster claw and knuckle, hand-cleaned in-house by hand — never machine-picked — then finished to order in one of two signature styles: the Connecticut Roll, tossed warm in brown butter, or the Maine Roll, served cold with a black garlic Old Bay mayo. The menu extends into a five-preparation raw oyster bar, Peruvian and Nikkei ceviches — including a recipe for leche de tigre passed down from a close friend's grandmother in Lima, known internally as "Tía Tati's" — and a caviar add-on program available on nearly everything on the menu, from $30.

Lobsteria operates nightly from 5 p.m. to 2 a.m. at 144 NE 27th Street in Miami's Midtown/Wynwood district, seating guests at lantern-strung outdoor tables around the vintage Airstream — one of the only premium seafood destinations in Miami open past midnight.

Despite never running a single ad or press outreach, Lobsteria has accumulated 145 five-star-leaning Google reviews and a 4.9 average rating entirely through word of mouth. "The first bite was insane," reads one representative review from Tony Lopez, a Google Local Guide — one of dozens describing it as the best lobster roll they've had outside of New England.

## About Lobsteria

Lobsteria is a premium Maine lobster roll, oyster, and ceviche concept operating from a converted vintage 1977 Airstream trailer in Miami's Wynwood neighborhood. Founded in 2019 by chef-owner Pau Casals, Lobsteria serves wild-caught Maine lobster claw and knuckle, hand-cleaned in-house, alongside a five-preparation raw oyster bar, Peruvian and Nikkei ceviches, and a caviar add-on program, nightly from 5 p.m. to 2 a.m. Learn more at lobsteria.co or @lobsteriamia on Instagram and TikTok.
