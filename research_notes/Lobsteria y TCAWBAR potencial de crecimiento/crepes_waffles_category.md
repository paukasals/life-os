## What is the estimated US market size and growth rate for crepes, waffles, or combined "crepe/waffle bar" concepts as a dedicated category?

### Takeaway
There is **no dedicated, credible, named-methodology market-size figure for "crepe/waffle bar" as a stand-alone US restaurant category** — IBISWorld, Technomic and Datassential all track waffles/crepes only as menu items inside broader categories (limited-service restaurants, "sweet treats," breakfast/brunch), not as their own NAICS industry. The numbers that exist as "market size" for crepes/waffles are almost all low-quality syndicated market-research-mill reports about kitchen *equipment* (crepe makers, waffle makers) or global packaged/frozen waffle products — not about the US restaurant/foodservice category — and should be treated as noise, not signal, for this question.

### Cited Findings
- IBISWorld's "Coffee & Snack Shops" industry (the closest umbrella category that would include a creperie/waffle bar as a light-meal/snack concept) is sized at **$75.5 billion in 2026**, with **94,331 US businesses**, growing at a **5.7% CAGR between 2021–2026** — but this is a broad category covering coffee shops, snack bars, smoothie/juice bars, etc., not crepes/waffles specifically. — [IBISWorld Coffee & Snack Shops](https://www.ibisworld.com/united-states/industry/coffee-snack-shops/1973/)
- A search for a dedicated IBISWorld NAICS code or report for "creperies" turned up nothing; library guides confirm IBISWorld organizes by NAICS 5-digit codes and crêperies would fall under general limited-service restaurant classifications, with no niche-specific report identified. — [Stanford IBISWorld guide](https://libguides.stanford.edu/library/ibisworld); [IBISWorld NAICS 311919](https://www.ibisworld.com/classifications/naics/311919/other-snack-food-manufacturing/)
- "Waffle Market size was valued at USD 4.2 Billion in 2024 & is estimated to reach USD 6.3 Billion by 2032, exhibiting a CAGR of 5.10% from 2025-2032" — but this is a **global, product-level market** (frozen waffles, waffle mix, waffle-making equipment), not a US foodservice/restaurant-category figure. Low-confidence syndicated market-research-mill source. — [Market Research Store](https://www.marketresearchstore.com/market-insights/waffle-market-827855)
- "Global waffle restaurant market was valued at $2.8 billion in 2024 and is forecasted to hit $5.4 billion by 2033, growing at a robust CAGR of 7.2%" vs. a conflicting figure from a different aggregator stating "global waffle restaurant market size reached USD 3.12 billion in 2024... ### Inferences
- There is effectively no independent "crepe/waffle bar as a category" market-sizing study from a tier-1 source (IBISWorld, Technomic, Datassential, National Restaurant Association). Anyone citing a specific US-dollar market size for "the crepe/waffle category" is almost certainly citing a market-research-mill product that bundles global packaged-food or equipment data, not US dedicated-concept restaurant revenue — this should be flagged explicitly to any investor/stakeholder.
- The ~8% menu-penetration figure for "crepes & pancakes" as a subcategory (Verdict Foodservice, H1 2024) is the most defensible proxy for "how present is this item on US menus," but it conflates crepes with pancakes (a much larger, more mainstream category), likely inflating the apparent penetration of crepes specifically.
- The absence of a dedicated NAICS/IBISWorld line for "creperies" or "waffle bars" itself is informative: it suggests the category is still small/fragmented enough that government and industry statisticians don't track it separately — consistent with an emerging-but-not-yet-institutionalized category, similar to where "poke bowl" or "boba tea" concepts sat several years before getting their own IBISWorld/Datassential tracking.

### Gaps
- No credible, named-methodology, US-only, dedicated-concept ("crepe/waffle cafe" as a restaurant format, not an ingredient/menu item) market-size-in-dollars figure was found. This is a genuine gap in available public data, not an oversight in search effort.
- Could not verify the Verdict Foodservice / GlobalData crepes & pancakes 8%-share figure's exact definition (share of what universe — all US restaurants? all breakfast concepts?) because the source page could not be fetched directly (egress-blocked); only the search-engine-generated summary was available.
- No IBISWorld paywalled report could be accessed to see if a more granular report (e.g., "Dessert & Pastry Shops" or similar) sizes waffles/crepes distinctly; this would require direct IBISWorld database access, which this research did not have.

## What is the historical/projected growth trend for this category (industry trend reports, Google Trends for "waffle bar," "crepe cafe," "liege waffle," etc., last 5–10 years)?

### Takeaway
Every named-source trend data point found (Yelp, Technomic, and food-trend-adjacent coverage) agrees the category is in a **genuine, currently-accelerating growth phase as of 2023–2025**, with creperies/waffle shops specifically named among the fastest-growing restaurant subtypes nationally — but this is a recent (1–2 year) acceleration, and I could not independently pull multi-year (5–10 year) Google Trends search-volume data because trends.google.com itself could not be queried/fetched in this environment; only secondary commentary about Trends data was available.

### Cited Findings
- Per Yelp's **State of the Restaurant Industry Report, 2024** (covering new business openings May 2023–April 2024): "Sweet shops experienced notable growth across several categories, such as dessert shops (up 52%), shaved ice (up 44%), pancakes (up 38%), waffles (up 32%), creperies (up 29%), ice cream and frozen yogurt (up 22%), and donuts (up 17%)." This is the single strongest hard-data point in this research: a named, dated, sourced (Yelp business-openings data) ranking that puts **waffles at +32% and creperies at +29% YoY in new-business openings** — both meaningfully above ice cream/frozen yogurt (22%) and donuts (17%), i.e., crepes and waffles are growing *faster* than more "mainstream" dessert categories, though slower than the broader "dessert shop" and "shaved ice" buckets. — [Yelp State of the Restaurant Industry 2024](https://trends.yelp.com/state-of-the-restaurant-industry-2024.html), as reported by [ModernRestaurantManagement: "Not Just for Breakfast: The Economic Power of Waffles"](https://modernrestaurantmanagement.com/not-just-for-breakfast-the-economic-power-of-waffles/) (primary site egress-blocked; captured via search snippet)
- Per the **2024 Technomic Breakfast Study**: waffles saw a **15.4% increase on restaurant appetizer menus** compared to 2023, reflecting a trend of waffles moving beyond breakfast into snacks/appetizers/desserts; separately, "40% [of consumers] have stated they are more likely to order waffles over any other breakfast menu item." — cited via search-engine summary of [Nation's Restaurant News coverage](https://www.nrn.com/supplier-news/explore-the-endless-opportunities-of-all-day-breakfast) (primary source not directly fetchable)
- "Liège waffles are showing double-digit year-over-year growth trends in the U.S., especially in food service locations like restaurants and cafes, which grew 39% over the last year," per The Kitchn's coverage of the Liège-waffle retail/foodservice trend — this is a named format (the "bar"-style, caramelized-sugar waffle most relevant to a crepes & waffles concept) showing the strongest cited growth rate of any specific sub-format found in this research. — [The Kitchn: "The Beloved European Breakfast Food... Taking Over Grocery Shelves"](https://www.thekitchn.com/liege-waffles-grocery-trends-23602709)
- "Dessert shop openings were up over 50% from May 2023 to April 2024, according to Yelp data, with related concepts like shaved ice up by double-digits" — restated independently by RestaurantDive in a piece asking whether dessert-concept growth is sustainable or a fad; the article also notes specific dessert-chain growth comparables (e.g., Rainbow Cone grew from 1 location to 20 in three years, with 30 more in development) as evidence of real multi-unit scaling momentum in the broader dessert-concept space that crepes/waffles sit within. — [RestaurantDive: "Dessert concepts are seeing record growth. But are they just a fad?"](https://www.restaurantdive.com/news/dessert-concepts-seeing-record-growth-but-are-they-just-a-fad/723871/) (egress-blocked; captured via search snippet)
- Placer.ai's Q1 2025 retail/dining visitation data notes that "the relative visit share of breakfast, coffee, bakeries and dessert shops also grew slightly" in Q1 2025, and separately that the fastest-growing *individual* chains nationally are concentrated in coffee/beverage/dessert (e.g., 7 Brew), indicating the broader "treat/beverage" dining occasion — which crepes/waffles compete within — continues to gain real foot-traffic share against traditional full-service dining, not just media hype. — [Placer.ai: "Retail & Dining Q1 2025 Recap"](https://www.placer.ai/anchor/articles/retail-dining-q1-2025-recap-budget-friendly-segments-shine); [Placer.ai: "Restaurant Success in 2025"](https://www.placer.ai/anchor/articles/restaurant-success-in-2025)

One tangential data point: general commentary notes "waffle-related searches consistently enjoy a high level of interest throughout the year, and rarely dip below the 70 [index] mark" when compared against pancake searches, with Minnesota, Utah, Vermont, New Hampshire, and Maine identified as the highest-search-interest "waffle states" — but this appears to be about the generic/evergreen term "waffle" (likely dominated by Eggo/breakfast-waffle search intent), not the "waffle bar" or dessert-concept framing relevant here, and the source itself did not specify a precise date range or Trends methodology. — [Fox News: "Are you in a pancake state or a waffle state"](https://www.foxnews.com/lifestyle/are-you-in-a-pancake-state-or-a-waffle-state) (low-rigor source; treat as color, not data)

### Inferences
- The convergence of three independent, named, dated sources (Yelp business-openings data, Technomic's Breakfast Study, and The Kitchn's Liège-waffle coverage) all pointing to double-digit-to-30%+ YoY growth specifically in waffle/creperie business openings and menu presence in 2023–2024 is a meaningfully strong signal — this is not just one aggregator's guess, it's triangulated across retail-trend journalism, a recognized foodservice research firm (Technomic), and a crowd-sourced local-business database (Yelp).
- The growth is concentrated in the **2023–2025 window** specifically (post-pandemic "treat economy" / affordable-indulgence dining trend), which is consistent with — and plausibly driven by — the same consumer behavior (budget-conscious small treats, Instagram/TikTok-driven discovery of visually distinctive foods like Liège waffles and crepe "flipping" videos) that is also benefiting boba tea, specialty cookies, and other visually shareable treat categories. This suggests the crepes/waffles growth may be riding a broader "shareable treat" wave rather than being a uniquely crepe/waffle-specific structural shift — relevant context for durability assessment.
- Lack of directly-verified long-run (5–10 year) Google Trends data is a real gap; the one-year 2023–2024 acceleration data available does not by itself prove a durable decade-long uptrend versus a short-term fad spike, and the RestaurantDive source explicitly frames this as an open question ("are they just a fad?").

### Gaps

This is a significant gap relative to the assignment's explicit ask and should be flagged to the report-writer; a human with direct Google Trends access should pull this before finalizing any "5–10 year trend" claim.
- No multi-year (pre-2023) Yelp or Technomic trend series for waffles/creperies specifically was found — only the single May 2023–April 2024 Yelp data point. Whether 2021–2023 showed similar growth, decline, or flat trends is unknown from available sources.

## Who are the known multi-unit or franchised crepe/waffle concepts in the US?

### Takeaway
There is a real but still small and fragmented set of multi-unit crepe and/or waffle concepts in the US — the most credible, actively-scaling franchise player is **Sweet Paris Creperie & Café** (21–27 units, aggressively franchising toward a stated 100-unit, 8-year goal); most other named brands (Crepe Delicious, Bruxie, CoCo Crêpes, Wafels & Dinges, Smaaken) are either shrinking, stuck at single-digit-to-low-double-digit units, regionally confined, or pivoting away from pure dine-in toward sandwich/other formats — none has achieved broad national scale on par with a category like boba tea or poke.

### Cited Findings
- **Sweet Paris Creperie & Café** (Houston-based, founded by Allison & Ivan Chavez): grew from **10 outlets in 2022 to 16 outlets in 2024** (franchised units growing from 6→11, company-owned from 4→5); as of year-end 2024, **39 signed-but-unopened franchise agreements across 10 states**. More recent (2025) sources show **21 stores in operation across Arizona, Florida, Minnesota, Texas, and Mexico**, with the brand's own site claiming **27 open and 26 more signed/in development**. Co-founders state a goal of **100 US units within 8 years (by ~2031)**, scaling from a base of ~12–21. Franchise Times' Top 400 ranking lists it at **#495** with **17 total US units** per that specific dataset (there is a genuine discrepancy across sources on the exact current count — ranging roughly 16–27 depending on date and whether signed-not-yet-open units are counted — but all sources agree on the same rapid-growth trajectory and the ~100-unit stated target). Format is **dine-in/fast-casual first**, not delivery-first, with a multi-unit franchise deal structure (e.g., a Virentes Partners Group deal covering Orlando, Jacksonville, Tampa Bay, Raleigh/Piedmont Triangle, and greater Nashville). — [Restaurant Dive franchise spotlight](https://www.restaurantdive.com/news/franchise-spotlight-sweet-paris-creperie-aims-for-100-units/642664/) (egress-blocked, captured via search snippet); [Franchise Times Top 400 #495](https://www.franchisetimes.com/top-400-2025/495-sweet-paris-creperie-cafe/article_6968cb06-d431-4f05-bc84-417fd2452ba5.html) (egress-blocked, captured via search snippet); [Entrepreneur Franchise Directory](https://www.entrepreneur.com/franchises/directory/sweet-paris-creperie-cafe/336372); [franchising.com: Sweet Paris Southern expansion](https://www.franchising.com/news/20240131_sweet_paris_crperie__caf_increases_southern_presence_with_multiunit_agreeme.html)
- **Crepe Delicious** (Canadian-founded, Concord, Ontario): per its most recent (2026) Franchise Disclosure Document, the system consists of **11 total units — 10 franchised, 1 company-owned**, with **9 units in the United States specifically (8 franchised, 1 company-owned)** as of 2025. This is a sharp **decline** from historical peak figures — older sources cite "over 65 locations open globally as of January 2021" and even "over 75 stores worldwide" more recently cited elsewhere — indicating the brand has **contracted significantly**, not grown, in its US/global footprint in recent years. Mostly mall-based format; also runs a "ghost kitchen"/delivery-only variant (e.g., "Crepe Delicious By Ghost Kitchens" listed on DoorDash in Burlington, Ontario), suggesting the brand has experimented with a delivery-first model as a lower-capex alternative to its mall-kiosk format. — [FDD Exchange: Crepe Delicious 2026](https://fddexchange.com/fdd/crepe-delicious-2025/); [FranchiseHelp](https://www.franchisehelp.com/franchises/crepe-delicious/); [DoorDash listing: Crepe Delicious By Ghost Kitchens, Burlington ON](https://www.doordash.com/business/crepe-delicious-by-ghost-kitchens-666401)
- **Bruxie** (Southern California-based "chicken & waffles" sandwich concept — waffle-as-bread rather than crepe/waffle-dessert format, but the closest US comparable for a waffle-centric multi-unit chain): recently changed ownership (TCGM Holding Company, owned by early investors Tommy Chua and Gordon Miles, bought the brand's assets after L Catterton — which took a majority stake in 2016 — exited). One source describes the chain as having **"seven locations, mostly in Southern California"** at time of the ownership change; another describes signed agreements for **18 new US locations** (California, Colorado, Alabama, Georgia, Florida) plus **24 international locations across three countries** (including a first South Korea unit). A 2025 Atlanta-market expansion attempt (Chamblee, GA, opened March 2024) **closed within about 10 months** (by January 2025), while a separate Athens, GA location opened — i.e., mixed/uneven execution on out-of-state expansion. Format is **dine-in/fast-casual first**, not delivery-first; headquarters relocated from Anaheim to Santa Ana with a new store prototype in Irvine. — [NRN: "With new ownership, waffle sandwich chain Bruxie gets ready to grow"](https://www.nrn.com/emerging-chains/with-new-ownership-waffle-sandwich-chain-bruxie-gets-ready-to-grow) (egress-blocked, captured via search snippet); [Tone to ATL: Bruxie Atlanta closure](https://www.tonetoatl.com/2025/01/Bruxie-Chicken-Waffles-Franchise-Closed-Chamblee-Atlanta.html); [Retail & Restaurant Facility Business: Bruxie Athens opening](https://retailrestaurantfb.com/bruxie-to-open-its-first-georgia-location-in-athens/)
- **7 Leaves Cafe** (not actually a crepe/waffle concept — it is a Vietnamese-American boba/coffee café with macarons; included here only because it surfaced in search and is sometimes confused with the category) grew from **16 outlets in 2018 to 42–43 units by 2024–2026** (14 franchised / 28 company-owned as of 2024). This is **not a direct comparable** for crepes/waffles but is a useful benchmark for how fast a well-run "treat café" format *can* scale in ~6–8 years when it achieves product-market fit — relevant as an aspirational growth-curve comparable, not a category peer. — [Restaurant Business Online Future 50 2024](https://www.restaurantbusinessonline.com/future-50-2024/7-leaves-cafe); [SharpSheets 7 Leaves Cafe FDD analysis](https://sharpsheets.io/blog/7-leaves-cafe-franchise-fdd-profits-costs-fees/)
- **CoCo Crêpes, Waffles & Coffee** (Houston, founded 2005 by chef Youssef Nafaa under Bella Restaurants Group): grown to **9 locations** in the Houston metro area, **2 corporate-owned, 7 franchisee-owned**, after launching franchising (franchise fee $40,000; estimated initial investment $431,000–$889,500). Actively seeking to expand "beyond Houston... into other Texas markets and beyond," but **still regionally confined to a single metro after ~20 years** — a useful cautionary comparable showing that a crepe/waffle concept can be a durable, modestly profitable multi-unit franchise without achieving wide geographic scale. Dine-in/fast-casual first (menu also includes omelets, soups, salads, paninis, smoothies — i.e., broader than a pure crepe/waffle dessert concept). — [Franchising.com: CoCo Crêpes Overview](https://www.franchising.com/cococrpeswafflescoffee/); [cococrepes.com franchising page](https://www.cococrepes.com/franchising/)
- **Wafels & Dinges** (NYC-based, founded 2007 by Belgian national Thomas DeGeest): began as food trucks/carts, evolved primarily into **food kiosks** at high-traffic NYC locations (Herald Square, Bryant Park, Flatiron, American Museum of Natural History) plus a food truck at Rockefeller Center. Announced intent in **2022 to "begin selective franchising nationwide"** beyond NYC, but no subsequent source found confirming any out-of-NYC franchised unit has actually opened as of 2025–2026 — i.e., a **stated-but-unrealized national franchising plan**, still effectively a single-metro (NYC) operation after ~18 years. Dine-in/walk-up/kiosk first, not delivery-first by original design (though NYC kiosks likely also serve delivery apps). — [RestaurantNews.com: Wafels & Dinges to begin selective franchising nationwide](https://www.restaurantnews.com/iconic-nyc-belgian-waffle-institution-wafels-dinges-to-begin-selective-franchising-nationwide-031122/); [wafels.com locations](https://wafels.com/pages/find-location?filterby=zipcode&value=new+york)
- **Smaller/local/non-scaled brands found but not meaningfully multi-unit nationally:** **Smaaken Waffle Sandwiches** (Portland, OR food-cart concept since 2012, still only **3 cart locations** in the Portland metro after 13+ years — essentially flat, not scaling); **The Crêpe Café** (BFC Retail Group, originally launched 2001 in Australia, now offering US franchising via thecrepecafe.com, but no US unit count could be confirmed from available sources); **Crêpinos** (crepinos.com, offers franchising, origin/unit count unconfirmed — likely non-US or early-stage); **Crepella Crepes & Waffles Cafe** (crepella.us) and **Motley Crepe Co.** (Chelsea-area, per thesuntimesnews.com) appear to be single-location independent shops, not multi-unit chains, based on available search results. **Call it Crepe** and **Crepini** (named in the assignment's suggested-brand list) could not be found at all in search results — either these are extremely small/defunct/misremembered brand names, or not indexed; this should be flagged as a gap rather than assumed non-existent. — [thecrepecafe.com/franchising](https://thecrepecafe.com/franchising); [crepinos.com/en/franchise](https://crepinos.com/en/franchise/); [Yelp: Smaaken Waffle Sandwiches, Portland](https://www.yelp.com/biz/smaaken-waffle-sandwiches-portland-2)
- **Crepes & Waffles** (the Colombian chain of the same name as the category, founded 1980 in Bogotá) — Wikipedia and Mayorga Coffee both describe it as a major Latin American chain with expansion into **Spain and Peru** and other countries, but **no US locations were identified** in any source found; appears to be a non-US comparable (relevant as a "proof of concept at scale" internationally, but not a US competitor or comparable multi-unit operator). — [Mayorga Coffee: "From Garage to Culinary Icon: How Crepes & Waffles Became Colombia's..."](https://mayorgacoffee.com/blogs/news/crepes-and-waffles)

### Inferences
- Across every named brand found, **Sweet Paris is the only concept showing real, accelerating, multi-state franchise scale-up momentum** (signed agreements across 10+ states, explicit 100-unit target, PE-adjacent partner deals). Every other named brand is either flat (Smaaken, CoCo Crêpes, Wafels & Dinges), shrinking (Crepe Delicious), or executing unevenly on expansion (Bruxie's Atlanta closure within 10 months). This suggests the category *can* scale with the right execution/capital/franchise-systemization (Sweet Paris proves the ceiling is not structurally capped), but that most operators to date have not managed to break out of single-metro or single-digit-unit status — consistent with a category that is still early/immature in franchise-system development, not one with an already-proven, widely-replicated playbook.
- None of the identified brands are described as primarily **delivery-first** in their core format — Sweet Paris, Bruxie, CoCo Crêpes, Wafels & Dinges are all dine-in/fast-casual/kiosk/walk-up first, with Crepe Delicious's ghost-kitchen variant being the one explicit delivery-first exception found (and notably, that's the variant of a *shrinking* brand). This is a meaningful signal for a delivery-first, food-truck-origin concept assessing expansion: the subject company's delivery-first model appears to have **few, if any, direct national-scale comparables** to benchmark against — it may be pioneering a distribution model within this category rather than following an established playbook.
- The wide variance in reported Sweet Paris unit counts (16 to 27, depending on source and date, plus "signed but not open" ambiguity) is a reminder that franchise-growth press releases often count signed deals as if they were open units; actual open-door unit count should be verified directly against the most recent FDD before using any Sweet Paris figure as a hard benchmark.

### Gaps
- Could not find "Call it Crepe" or "Crepini" as US multi-unit or franchise brands in any search result — these may not exist under those exact names, may be defunct, or may be mis-specified in the original brand list; flagging explicitly rather than guessing.
- Could not access Crepe Delicious's, Sweet Paris's, or CoCo Crêpes' full FDD Item 20 (unit count/opening/closing tables) directly — all figures above are secondhand via FDD-summary aggregator sites (FDD Exchange, FranchiseHelp, FranchiseGrade), which can be imprecise or dated; a human with direct FDD access should verify before using these numbers in financial modeling.
- No delivery-mix data (% of sales via UberEats/DoorDash vs. dine-in) was found for any named brand — none of the sources discussed delivery as a % of revenue for Sweet Paris, Bruxie, CoCo Crêpes, or any other concept.

## How does the category perform specifically on food delivery apps (UberEats/DoorDash) — is dessert/waffles/crepes a growing order category in platform trend reports?

### Takeaway

## Is this category "exotic" (needs market education) or already broadly familiar/adopted in the US — more like a burger/taco, or more like sushi circa 2010?

### Takeaway
Available evidence suggests crepes and waffles sit **closer to the "familiar, low-education-needed" end of the spectrum than to sushi-circa-2010**, but are not as universally mainstream as burgers/tacos either — waffles in particular are already a deeply familiar American breakfast food (no raw-fish-style psychological barrier), while crepes are familiar as a *concept* (most Americans know what a crepe is, unlike sushi pre-California-roll) but are less frequently eaten/ordered day-to-day than pancakes or waffles, making the category more like an established-but-still-growing niche (closer to "artisan donut shop" or "specialty grilled cheese" than to either burgers or 2010 sushi).

### Cited Findings
- Historical framing of sushi's US adoption: "Initially, the thought of consuming raw fish was daunting and unappealing to the majority of non-Japanese Americans, so sushi remained relatively obscure and was not immediately embraced by the wider population... as sushi chefs began to educate consumers on the variety and complexity of sushi options available, the tide began to turn," with the California roll cited as the key innovation that mainstreamed it. This confirms sushi's historical adoption *did* require a specific education/de-risking strategy (the California roll as a "training wheels" product) — a strategy crepes/waffles arguably do not need, since neither involves an unfamiliar raw ingredient or unfamiliar eating mechanic. — [sushikingnm.com: "How Sushi Became a Staple in US Gastronomy"](https://www.sushikingnm.com/how-sushi-became-a-staple-in-us-gastronomy.html); [Harvard DASH: "Red, White, And Sushi"](https://dash.harvard.edu/server/api/core/bitstreams/7ddfca4a-504b-4923-aa38-bf7cdf4c2c76/content)
- By contrast, a search specifically comparing crepes to mainstream American food found: "crêperies had barely made their way to American cities at this point, much less to America's heartland... crêpes remain less integrated into mainstream American cuisine compared to sushi, though crêpe cakes and brunch-worthy crêpes with indulgent toppings have become trendy staples in the United States" — i.e., even a direct AI-search attempt to find evidence that crepes are "less mainstream than sushi" could not produce a clear historical parallel; the search instead surfaced evidence that crepes are already appearing as "trendy staples," suggesting the sushi-style multi-decade education arc doesn't apply the same way. — [search synthesis, no single primary source isolated; see ICE: "Origin of Crepes"](https://www.ice.edu/blog/french-crepes) and [Escoffier: "What Is a Crepe?"](https://www.escoffier.edu/blog/baking-pastry/what-is-a-crepe/)
- Waffles' familiarity is further evidenced by their sheer ubiquity as a breakfast staple (Eggo, Waffle House, IHOP, etc. predate any "trend" framing by decades) and by the Technomic Breakfast Study finding that 40% of consumers already say they're more likely to order waffles over any other breakfast item — strong evidence of deep pre-existing familiarity/preference, not a category needing introduction. — [cited via NRN coverage of 2024 Technomic Breakfast Study](https://www.nrn.com/supplier-news/explore-the-endless-opportunities-of-all-day-breakfast)
- The Yelp openings data (29% creperie growth, 32% waffle growth YoY, per the prior section) itself is indirect evidence against a "needs heavy education" framing: categories that require significant consumer education to adopt (e.g., early-2000s sushi, or current-day more exotic imports) typically show slower, more geographically clustered (e.g., coastal/urban-only) growth than the broad-based, double-digit, nationally-reported growth seen here for waffles/creperies.

### Inferences
- The most defensible framing, synthesizing all findings: **waffles need essentially zero market education** in the US (it's one of the most universally familiar foods there is — more like a burger than like sushi), while **crepes need only mild/light education**, mainly around format and occasion (is it a dessert? a meal? a snack?) and around *specific* styles (e.g., a "Liège waffle" or a "galette" vs. a sweet crepe) rather than the base concept itself. This is meaningfully different from sushi circa 2000–2010, where the barrier was visceral (raw fish) and required a true product-innovation workaround (California roll) to cross; crepes/waffles have no equivalent visceral barrier.
- A "crepe/waffle bar" concept's growth constraint is therefore more likely to be **discovery, distribution, and habitual-ordering-occasion formation** (i.e., getting people to think of crepes/waffles as a thing they order via delivery app on a Tuesday night, the way they already think of pizza or boba) rather than **basic category education** (teaching people what a crepe or waffle is). This is a materially more favorable starting position for national expansion than a true "sushi in 2010"-style exotic category would be.
- Given the category's recent growth (2023–2025) is concentrated and fast (29–39% YoY by various measures) rather than slow-and-steady over a decade, this looks more like a "newly-discovered-but-already-familiar" dynamic — similar to how birria tacos or Dubai chocolate went viral on already-familiar food bases (taco, chocolate bar) rather than requiring audiences to learn an unfamiliar eating paradigm from scratch.
